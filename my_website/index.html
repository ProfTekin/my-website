01.1 / THE BUILDING BLOCKS

Meet your network.

A network connects devices so they can exchange data and share resources. Each piece of hardware has a different job.

LOCAL DELIVERY

Switch

Connects devices within a LAN. Learns MAC addresses and forwards known unicast frames to the correct port.

See it in school

The classroom PCs connect to a switch. Unknown destinations and broadcasts are flooded to other ports within the same VLAN.

REPEAT EVERYTHING

Hub

A legacy physical-layer device. Repeats incoming signals to every other port; connected devices share bandwidth and a collision domain.

See it in school

If one PC sends data, every other connected port receives the signal. Hubs are largely replaced by switches.

BETWEEN NETWORKS

Router

Forwards IP packets between networks using a routing table. Often acts as the default gateway.

See it in school

Your school router connects the school LAN to the internet. A home “router” often also includes a switch, Wi-Fi and a firewall.

SHARED STORAGE

NAS

Network-attached storage: a device that provides files over a network, with user accounts and permissions.

See it in school

Students save project files in a shared folder. RAID can improve availability, but is not a substitute for a separate backup.
